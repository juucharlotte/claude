THE NEUROSCIENCE OF LUCK:
The Neuroscience of Luck: Luck feels random, but your brain plays a major role in shaping how often you experience it. What you call luck is often the result of how you perceive, process, and respond to your environment. Two people can be in the same situation, yet one notices opportunity while the other doesn't.
That difference is neurological.
Your Brain Is Constantly Filtering Reality: Your brain filters an overwhelming amount of information through the Reticular Activating System. This determines what reaches your awareness. When you're oriented toward curiosity and openness, you notice more opportunities. When you're stressed or rigid, your perception narrows and you miss them.
Pattern Recognition Creates Opportunity: Your brain is constantly identifying patterns and predicting outcomes through the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus. What feels like luck is often your ability to recognize and act on subtle signals before others do.
Dopamine Drives "Lucky" Behavior: dopamine drives exploration, motivation, and risk-taking. When it's balanced, you're more likely to try new things and engage with uncertainty, which increases your exposure to chance opportunities.
Stress Shrinks Your Luck Surface Area: Under stress, the amygdala takes over, narrowing your focus and reducing risk-taking. This limits your environment and decreases the likelihood of unexpected positive outcomes.
What This Means for You: Luck is shaped by how your brain filters, connects, and responds to the world
